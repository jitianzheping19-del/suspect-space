# Lobby arena update

Soccer setup is now part of the 3D court. The scoreboard has larger solid digits, a start/stop station below it, and blue/red bot count stations on either side. Walk up and tap the object or use the contextual action button. Only the host can start, stop, or adjust bot counts; counts are shared before kickoff and locked during the match. The exit station is beside the entrance. The former soccer setup and score action overlays are hidden.

The standard pitch is 20 × 30 (large: 21.4 × 38; extra large: 22 × 44). Goals are 6.4 wide and 2.5 tall. The soccer camera follows the player and nearby ball with a fixed angle and limited ball offset; camera preferences and orbit gestures cannot override it. The lobby starts with a more distant third-person view.

Attack and defense occupy the same two button slots and are mutually exclusive. Without possession, an opponent holding the ball gives press/slide; loose balls give dash/slide. Matchup is removed from the controls and tutorial. Double-tap shoots in the current facing direction from any range, or flick the second tap to aim. Flick toward a teammate and hold for 350 ms to send a through pass, including when the teammate is aligned with the goal. Feint flicks are interpreted relative to the character's facing: forward double touch, side roulette, backward heel lift. Long press remains dash. Bot passing retains its airborne pass decision for obstructed lanes.

Character turns have angular acceleration and reduced movement speed during sharp turns. Pressure steps include planted feet and shoulder/hip weight. Slide, feint and overhead pose interpolation preserves velocity through intermediate keyframes without overshoot. Outfits follow torso poses and restore cleanly after rendering. Ball rendering uses the same gravity, drag and spin integrator as the match plus a damped correction spring; rolling derives from visible travel. Keeper reaction is 210 ms, with reduced lateral adjustment and dive reach.

Goal presentation shows only the score sliding over the game, with a transparent background. The tutorial opens on pointer down, contains no question mark or matchup category, and autoplays muted inline clips. The loading screen has orbital rings, stars and a luminous progress bar, with reduced-motion support. Existing cache/readiness behavior is retained.

Twenty original outfits were added: 10 legend-tier premium outfits and 10 golden-tier outfits. Existing item numbers are stable. Catalog, ownership validation, embedded character builders and previews are synchronized. Every new outfit owns/disposes its geometry/materials, uses the existing animation loop, and has mobile geometry limits.

Validation includes mobile touch at 390×844, 568×320 and 1024×768, host-only setup, world controls/camera/turning in the actual 3D scene, score animation, loading lifecycle, all tutorial videos, delayed/duplicated network delivery, 2/6/20-player simulations, kick/air/slide/feint physics, and all 20 skin models. Chromium uses software WebGL for scene checks. Physical iOS hardware was not available. The GAS exporter uses the published tutorial media URL so playback works outside the Pages origin.
